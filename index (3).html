Overview

What is Grokking?

Grokking is a phenomenon where a neural network first perfectly memorises the training set, then — after a long delay with no change in training loss — suddenly generalises to unseen data.

This project reproduces and extends Progress measures for grokking via mechanistic interpretability (Nanda et al., ICLR 2023), using prime p = 67 as a distinct test case, with six fully-implemented analysis parts including a novel co-grokking experiment in which a single model learns both addition and multiplication simultaneously.

The one-layer transformer trained here learns modular arithmetic not by memorising lookup tables, but by implementing a genuine Fourier-rotation algorithm that is fully reverse-engineerable from its weights.

Six-Part Analysis

Results

Part 1

Grokking Curves: (a + b) mod 67

Training accuracy reaches 100% at ~epoch 500. Validation accuracy stays near 0% until epoch ≈ 10,750, then jumps sharply — the textbook grokking signature.

Part 2

Sparse Fourier Basis (Addition & Multiplication)

A 1-D DFT of the token embedding matrix and post-ReLU MLP activations reveals that both representations concentrate power on a sparse set of frequencies, confirming the model implements a Fourier-rotation algorithm rather than a lookup table.

Addition — dominant frequencies ω ∈ {4, 6, 26, 27}

Multiplication — dominant frequencies ω ∈ {11, 21, 33} (in Z₆₆, not Z₆₇)

Part 3

Three-Phase Grokking Decomposition

Tracking four metrics jointly exposes three mechanistically distinct phases:

① Memorisation

Train acc → 100%; test acc ≈ 0%; weights grow unchecked.

② Circuit Formation

Fourier circuits strengthen; weight norm begins to fall.

③ Cleanup

Weight decay prunes memorisation; test acc jumps to 100%.

Part 4

Epochs-Until-Generalisation vs. Data Fraction

Models trained with fractions from 10% to 90% of all 67² pairs reveal a sharp data threshold for grokking:

10 – 30%✗ No grokking within 15,000 epochs

Part 5

Algorithm Reverse-Engineering: (a × b) mod 67

For multiplication, the model learns the discrete-logarithm isomorphism:

(Z₆₇*, ×)  ≅  (Z₆₆, +)   via   a ↦ ind_g(a)   where g = 2

Learned algorithm — 3 steps:

  • 1
    Encode — embed token a as Fourier features of its discrete log.
  • 2
    Combine — compute log_g(a) + log_g(b) mod 66 via the cosine addition formula.
  • 3
    Decode — map back via 2^(log_g(a)+log_g(b) mod 66) mod 67.
Part 6

Co-Grokking: Addition + Multiplication Simultaneously

A single model trained on both tasks at once shows co-grokking: addition groks at ep 31,150 and multiplication at ep 32,950 — a gap of only 1,800 epochs, suggesting shared internal Fourier representations between the two operations.
